1.15 Are there procedures or work instructions available to the heat treat personnel that define the heat treating process?
  • There shall be procedures or work instructions available to all employees involved in heat treating and inspection of heat treated product.
  • These procedures or work instructions shall include methods of addressing potential emergencies (such as power failure), equipment start-up, equipment shut-down, product segregation (see 2.8), product inspection, and general operating procedures.

The review interval, the availability check interval and the time allowed to train after a new revision are site rules, not CQI-9 figures. The typical documents are drafts for a direct-fired gas pusher line with an oil quench, a controlled cooling chamber and a tempering chamber: adapt them to the furnace manuals, the site's emergency plan and the way the line is really run before approving them.

Method
  • Define the process: the register lists every procedure and work instruction with the topics it covers. Each furnace needs, in force, a general operating procedure, a start-up and a shut-down instruction and an emergency instruction for a power failure (a gas / flame failure instruction is recommended for every gas-fired line and a fire instruction for the site); the site needs a product segregation procedure (see HTSA 2.8) and a product inspection procedure. A document with no furnace ticked applies to every furnace.
  • Controlled: approved by name (the approval locks it), reviewed at least every review interval (“Record review” when it is still right), changed only through a new revision with the reason for the change; earlier revisions are kept.
  • Available at the point of use: each document in force names the places where it is kept (furnace control panel, loading station, laboratory, quarantine area, screen). An availability check at each place confirms the current revision is there and legible; an earlier revision, a missing or illegible copy, or an uncontrolled copy left in place is a finding. After a new revision the places are checked again.
  • Available to all employees involved: every employee in heat treating and inspection is listed with the roles they work in. Each document names the roles that use it, so the matrix shows who must have read and understood which revision. Training on the current revision is due within the time set after its approval (or after the employee started in the role).
