HTSA 1.6 · Documented system to create process specifications

The requirement, the evidence this register holds for it and the resulting assessment. Confirm the wording and the scoring rules against the controlled CQI-9 copy.

1.6 Is there a documented system to create process specifications for all active processes?A documented system for creating process specifications is necessary for operating the heat treat process within the desired, requested process parameters to reach the final product specifications.
  • Authorization shall be defined to a responsible person (see 1.1) for establishing process specifications for the heat treatment of the products with the available equipment.
  • The heat treater shall have written process specifications for all active processes and identify all steps of the process including relevant process parameters.
  • Parameters shall have operating tolerances as defined by the organization in order to maintain process control.
  • Process specifications shall be available in the form of work instructions, job card, computer-based recipes, or other similar documents.
  • All process specification changes shall be reviewed to the extent necessary to ensure continued conformity with customer requirements for process changes.
  • All process specification changes shall be documented to include the date the process specification change was implemented and the person(s) approving the change.

Specification register

One controlled specification per part and process, with revision, approval and periodic review. An approved revision is locked; a change is made as a new revision, and the approved one stays in force until the new revision is approved.

Method
  • CQI-9 4th Edition: hardening & tempering (with the quench) is covered by Process Table A; normalizing and isothermal annealing by Process Table E. Confirm the table letters and every numeric requirement against the controlled copy of CQI-9 and the customer-specific requirements.
  • A specification holds the part, steel grade, process, the furnaces it is approved for, the load, the set value and tolerance of every zone, the push time, the quench or controlled-cooling parameters, the quality requirements with sampling, and the approval.
  • Time per zone = tray positions of the zone × push time. Time through the line per tray = (13 + 11) × push time: measured, a tray leaves the hardening chamber 13 pushes after it entered and the tempering chamber 11 pushes later (setting), at a push, so the time between the chambers shortens the last tempering zone instead of adding to the total. Hardening chamber 13 positions (5 + 4 + 4), tempering chamber 12 (4 + 4 + 4). Between the chambers: hardening & tempering = quench delay + dip time + transfer; isothermal annealing = the controlled cooling cycle: at each push the extractor takes the 13th tray out of the hardening chamber (and the 26th out of the tempering chamber); the tray goes on the rail into the controlled cooling chamber, air is blown for the cooling time, then it is pushed into the tempering chamber. Cooling time + transfers (setting) must end before the next push, because the controlled cooling chamber holds one tray; normalizing = transfer.
  • The line checks (hardening chamber above 800 °C for hardening and isothermal annealing, 700–750 °C preheat for normalizing, tempering below the austenitizing set value) follow this line's practice; they are warnings, not CQI-9 requirements.
  • The furnaces are direct-fired without a protective atmosphere, so scale and surface decarburization are expected: specify the decarburization limit and check it.
  • HTSA 1.6: only an authorized person approves a specification (inside CALOR the heat treat person of HTSA 1.1 and the positions marked on the HT organization page, otherwise the names in Settings). Every step is listed with its parameters and an operating tolerance (± value, a maximum, or a range); the approval is refused while one is missing. Each specification states where it is available at the line (work instruction, job card, recipe) and prints as a job card. A new revision needs a change review (customer requirements, customer notification, control plan and PFMEA, validation) before approval, and the date it was implemented on the line is recorded after approval. Inside CALOR every part produced on the Daily production page in the last days must have a specification in force for its furnace.
