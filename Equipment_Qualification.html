CQI-9 · Pyrometry · Furnace / equipment survey and qualification

Equipment qualification

Furnaces

One qualification record per chamber: what the equipment is (survey of the equipment), how it is classified (furnace class, instrumentation type), and whether it is qualified today. Select a chamber to show only its record.

Qualification status

A chamber is qualified when its equipment record is approved, its operating ranges pass the temperature uniformity survey, every control thermocouple has a valid SAT and is within its service life, the instruments are calibrated, the over-temperature protection was tested, the furnace class meets the tightest tolerance of the parts it runs, the assigned instrumentation type is supported by the installed hardware, and no equipment change waits for requalification.

Equipment gaps

Gaps found from the equipment survey against the instrumentation type and good pyrometry practice, with the action decided for each. Gaps are recorded honestly; an action can be a capital project.

Equipment changes and requalification

Changes that can affect temperature uniformity or measurement (rebuild, burners, refractory, thermocouple relocation, controller or recorder) need a new TUS and / or SAT before the chamber is qualified again.

Settings, furnace classes, instrumentation types and data

Furnace classes

Uniformity tolerance of each class. Defaults are the usual six classes (±3 … ±28 °C); confirm them against the controlled CQI-9 copy.

Instrumentation types

What each type requires and the SAT and TUS intervals that go with it. Defaults are a starting point modelled on the usual A–E scheme; confirm the requirements and intervals against the controlled CQI-9 copy and the customer requirements.

Data

Method
  • Each chamber is surveyed (type, heating, atmosphere, zones, sensors, control, recording, over-temperature protection, work zone, quench) and classified: the furnace class follows from the uniformity tolerance its qualified ranges hold; the instrumentation type is assigned against what is installed, never higher.
  • The required class follows from the tightest set-value tolerance of the process specifications in force for the chamber; a chamber qualified to a looser class than its parts need is a gap.
  • Live status is taken from the other CALOR pages: TUS ranges (Uniformity), SAT and element life (Thermocouple SAT), quench tests (Quench media) and specifications. Standalone, those items show as not available.
  • Class tolerances, type requirements and intervals are editable defaults to be confirmed against the controlled CQI-9 copy.
