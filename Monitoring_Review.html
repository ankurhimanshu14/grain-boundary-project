HTSA 1.9 · Monitoring system reviewed by qualified personnel

The requirement, the evidence this page holds for it and the resulting assessment. Confirm the wording and the scoring rules against the controlled CQI-9 copy.

1.9 Is the heat treat monitoring system reviewed by Qualified Personnel?This review is intended to be a second level review in addition to those performed by the heat treat operators. This review would be performed by qualified personnel as defined per question 1.17. The heat treat monitoring system includes but is not limited to temperature strip charts, atmosphere strip charts, computer data logs, furnace and operator logs, etc.
  • Qualified Personnel shall review the furnace monitoring systems at intervals not to exceed 24 hours.
  • The process of reviewing the furnace data shall be documented. This requirement also applies to computerized data.
  • This second level review shall include detection and reaction to out of control conditions or alarms. This reaction shall be documented.

Settings and data

The 24-hour limit is the CQI-9 maximum; the warning window and the look-back period are site rules, not CQI-9 figures. The procedure number is the documented review process (the work instruction or procedure that says how the furnace data is reviewed).

Monitoring systems reviewed

The records the reviewer looks at for every furnace. Each ticked system is answered in every review.

Data

The reviewers and the reviews are kept in this browser only. Alarms, SCADA day summaries, quench tests and push-cycle holds are read from the CALOR pages that record them (Alarms, SCADA review, Quench media, Daily production) in the same browser. Export a backup regularly.

Method
  • Who: a second-level review, on top of the operators' own checks, by a reviewer qualified as per HTSA 1.17 (listed with the evidence of the qualification).
  • How often: every furnace in operation is reviewed so that no more than 24 hours pass between two reviews, and the periods of data reviewed follow each other without a gap. A furnace stopped by a recorded shutdown (Alarms page) needs no review until it starts again.
  • What: every monitoring system in the list — the computerized data log (SCADA) and temperature charts, alarm history, atmosphere / combustion, quench, push cycle and the operator and furnace logs — is answered: in control, out of control or not applicable with the reason.
  • Detect and react: the conditions already recorded in CALOR for the period (alarms, thermocouples out of tolerance on a SCADA day, quench tests out of limits, push-cycle holds) are listed in the review and each is acknowledged. Every out-of-control condition gets a documented reaction: what was done, the product disposition, the owner, and its closure with the verification.
