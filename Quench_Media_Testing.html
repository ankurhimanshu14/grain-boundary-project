How a test moves through PDCA

  • Plan — the test plan sets each parameter's method, frequency and limits per quenchant; the schedule shows what is due.
  • Do — take the sample or reading and record the values (shift checks, weekly checks, laboratory report).
  • Check — every value is judged against its limits; values close to a limit are flagged before they fail.
  • Act — an out-of-limit result needs containment, root cause, correction, product-impact assessment and a passing re-test.

Test plan per quenchant

Method, frequency (days) and limits of each parameter. The defaults are typical values and must be confirmed against the controlled copy of CQI-9 (process table for the quench) and the quenchant supplier's data sheet. Leave a limit empty for a one-sided limit; with both empty the value is recorded but not judged.

Method
  • CQI-9 requires the quenchant to be tested and controlled: temperature and agitation are checked continuously or every shift, and the quenchant itself is analysed periodically (oil: water content, viscosity, flash point, acid number, sludge, cooling curve; polymer: concentration, cooling curve, contamination). Frequencies and limits here are editable defaults to be confirmed against the controlled CQI-9 copy and the supplier's data sheet.
  • A value outside its limits fails the test. A value within the warning margin (default 10 % of the allowed band, or of the limit for a one-sided limit) passes but is flagged.
  • A parameter is due when its last result is older than its frequency; daily checks are due every day.
  • Product at risk for an out-of-limit result is the time window from the last passing result of that parameter until the passing re-test, in line with the SAT register and the SCADA review.
