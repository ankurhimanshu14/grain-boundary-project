1.8 Does the heat treater collect and analyze data over time, and react to this data?The analysis of product characteristics (e.g. tensile strength) and process parameters (e.g. temperature) over time can yield vital information for defect prevention efforts. Examples include but are not limited to product property trend charts, scrap trends, and variation in process parameter recordings.
  • The organization shall have a system to collect, analyze, and react to product or process data over time.
  • Methods of analysis shall include ongoing trend or historical data analysis of product characteristics or process parameters.
  • The organization shall determine which parameters are included in such analysis.

Analysis plan: the parameters analysed

Signals and reactions

Every signal on a chart since the parameter's last review, and the actions decided in the reviews that are not closed yet. A signal is answered by a review of the parameter: an action with owner and date, “watch” with a reason, or no action with a reason.

Settings and data

The control limits are worked out from the first points of each parameter (the baseline) as an individuals chart: mean ± 3 × the average moving range ÷ 1.128. Signals follow the usual run rules (a point beyond the control limits, 8 points in a row on one side of the mean, 6 points in a row rising or falling, 2 of 3 points beyond 2 sigma on one side) and any point outside the specification. The window, the baseline size and the review interval are site rules, not CQI-9 figures.

Data

The plan, the reviews and the data entered here are kept in this browser only. The other data is read from the CALOR pages that record it (hardness, SCADA review, quench media, SAT, alarms, daily production) in the same browser. Export a backup regularly.

Method
  • Determine the parameters: the analysis plan lists the product characteristics and process parameters the organization analyses, where their data comes from, how often the chart is reviewed and who owns it. It is approved; a parameter added, changed or removed afterwards asks for a new approval.
  • Collect: the data is taken from the records already kept in CALOR — hardness tests (surface, core, decarburization, nonconforming rate), the SCADA review's day summaries (share of readings in tolerance, mean deviation of a thermocouple, difference between the two walls of a zone), quench media tests, SAT differences, alarms and rework — or entered here for data from other systems (tensile strength, scrap, customer returns).
  • Analyse: each parameter is shown as an individuals chart over the last window of days, with the mean and control limits of its baseline and the specification limits. A point outside the specification, beyond the control limits, a shift or a trend is a signal.
  • React: each parameter is reviewed at its interval and whenever it signals. The review records what the data shows and the decision: an action (owner, due date, done, verified), watch, or no action with the reason.
