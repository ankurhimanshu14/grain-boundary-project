HTSA 2.5 · Trap points in the heat treat process

The requirement, the evidence this page holds for it and the resulting assessment. Confirm the wording and the scoring rules against the controlled CQI-9 copy.

2.5 Is there a system to identify trap points in the entire heat treat process to reduce risk of mixed parts (inappropriate, non-heat treated, or improperly heat treated parts)?Heat-treating furnaces and other processing equipment (including but not limited to baskets, conveyors, chutes, etc.) contain areas that have a risk of trapping or holding parts. Such trapping of parts can lead to damage, improperly processed parts or lot mixing / contamination.
  • The heat treater shall have procedures to identify and monitor trap points for each process / equipment.
  • Monitoring of potential trap points shall occur for every lot changeover.

Lot changeovers

Changeover checks

At every lot changeover each trap point that applies to the outgoing and the incoming lot is checked and cleared before the new lot can mix with parts left behind. Parts found in a trap point stop the changeover: they are identified, segregated and dispositioned, and a reaction plan closes the check.

Method
  • Identify: the trap point register is the procedure. Every place on the line where a part can be left behind is listed per area and process: how parts get trapped, how it is monitored, how often and what to do with parts found. Each process route needs its trap points (loading, hardening chamber, quench / controlled cooling / transfer, tempering chamber, unloading, trays). The register is approved with a revision; a change after the approval asks for a new approval.
  • Monitor at every lot changeover: inside CALOR a changeover is found in the Daily production tray log whenever a tray brings a lot (part + batch, or part + heat when the batch is not available) that the tray before did not have. Each changeover needs a check of the trap points that apply to the outgoing and the incoming lot, matched by furnace and incoming lot within a day.
  • Hot areas (the hearth of the hardening and tempering chambers) cannot be looked into while the line runs: they are monitored by an empty separator tray between the lots and by reconciling the pieces of the outgoing lot (pieces loaded on the Daily production page against pieces counted at unloading), and inspected at every shutdown.
  • Parts found in a trap point, or a piece count that does not reconcile, make the check not OK: the parts are identified (which lot), segregated and dispositioned, both lots are contained, and a reaction plan (cause, action, owner, date) closes it.
