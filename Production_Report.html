CQI-9 · production record and traceability

Production report

Batches

One entry per batch. Choosing the part no. fills in its process specification; enter the batch, heat, times and quantity from the register. Trays, weight, time in the furnace and the expected exit time are calculated.

Tray loading log

One line per tray in the order the trays enter the hardening chamber, starting with the first tray of the day. Each tray enters one push time after the one before it, so only its contents are entered: one batch, parts of several batches (a mixed tray at the end of a batch) or nothing (an empty tray). When the push time is changed, enter the time of the first push at the new push time. A tray that entered off the cycle (for example after a stop) gets its actual time. The batches above then take their entry, exit, trays and zone windows from this log.

Totals of the day

Per part, per heat and per process for the furnace and date chosen above.

Approval

When every batch is complete, the supervisor approves the report. It is then locked; unlocking needs a reason and is recorded.

Process specifications

One specification per part no.; it is the same in every furnace. Set values per zone of the hardening and tempering chamber, push time, cooling times, loading per tray and part weight.

Search by heat no. or batch no.

Every batch of every day and furnace that matches.

Settings and data

Tray positions per zone are written as three numbers, e.g. 5,4,4 (Zone 1, 2, 3). Times between the chambers: hardening & tempering = quench delay + dip time + transfer to the tempering chamber; isothermal annealing = the controlled cooling cycle: transfer into the controlled cooling chamber + the part's controlled cooling time + transfer into the tempering chamber (the transfers are set here), all within the same push cycle; normalizing and full annealing = the values set here.

How the times are worked out
  • Trays = quantity ÷ loading per tray (rounded up). One tray moves one position per push; the first tray enters the hardening chamber at the entry time and each further tray one push later.
  • The line: hardening chamber 13 tray positions (5 + 4 + 4), tempering chamber 12 (4 + 4 + 4). At every push the extractor takes the 13th tray out of the hardening chamber and a new tray is pushed in; at the same time the 26th tray is taken out of the tempering chamber. For isothermal annealing the extracted tray goes on the rail into the controlled cooling chamber (one tray), air is blown for the part's controlled cooling time, and the tray is then pushed into the tempering chamber, all before the next push. Example: push time 18 min = transfer in + 12 min cooling + transfer out, the rest idle. The controlled cooling chamber is not a tray position of the line: 13 + 1 + 12 = 26 trays are in the line right after a push.
  • Expected exit from the tempering chamber = entry + (trays − 1 + tray positions of the hardening chamber + tray positions of the tempering chamber) × push time + minutes between the chambers (hardening & tempering: quench delay + dip + transfer; isothermal annealing: controlled cooling time + transfers; normalizing / full annealing: the settings). The entry is flagged when the recorded exit differs by more than the set number of push times.
  • Implied push time = (exit − entry − minutes between the chambers) ÷ (trays − 1 + all tray positions). It shows what push time the recorded times correspond to.
  • With the tray loading log: tray n + 1 enters one push time after tray n; a push change takes effect at its time (the first push at the new push time); a tray with an actual time starts the cycle again from that time. A tray in position k of the line moves on when the k-th tray after it enters, so the trays of the next day's log (or, when there is none yet, the push time in force) give the time each tray leaves a zone and the chamber. Leaving the tempering chamber = entry of the tray (positions of both chambers) trays later + the minutes between the chambers. For isothermal annealing the cooling cycle of each tray must end before the next push; a tray whose next push came earlier is flagged.
  • Zone windows: from the arrival of the first tray to the departure of the last tray of the batch in each zone. They are the basis for tracing batches in a temperature excursion.
  • Normalizing uses the hardening chamber as preheating (about 700–750 °C) and austenitizes in the tempering chamber; fan cooling, when required, follows the exit and does not change the exit time. Controlled cooling of isothermal annealing happens between the chambers.
