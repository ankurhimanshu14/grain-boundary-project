IATF 16949 · CQI-9 · Process FMEA and control plan

Control plan and PFMEA

How the two documents work together

  • PFMEA — one per process (hardening & tempering, isothermal annealing, normalizing) on this line, in the 7 steps of the AIAG & VDA FMEA Handbook: planning, structure (steps and 4M work elements), function, failure (effect, mode, cause), risk (S / O / D, Action Priority), optimization and results.
  • Control plan — one per part, made from its process specification: every product and process characteristic with its tolerance, method, sample, frequency, control and reaction plan, and the CALOR page that holds the records.
  • Links — the control plan names its PFMEA; steps of the PFMEA missing from the control plan, a control plan made from an older specification revision, and failures since the PFMEA was approved are flagged for review.

Document register

Approved documents are locked; a change is made as a new revision. Each document is reviewed periodically and whenever the process changes or a failure occurs.

PFMEA actions

Recommended actions of all PFMEA rows with Action Priority High or Medium. High-priority rows need an action (or a recorded justification) before the PFMEA is approved.

Settings and data

The PFMEA follows the AIAG & VDA FMEA Handbook, 1st edition (2019), the current edition. The Action Priority table built into this page (severity first, then occurrence, then detection) and the abbreviated rating criteria in the S / O / D lists must be verified against your copy of the handbook and the customer-specific requirements. RPN is not used: the handbook replaces it with the Action Priority.

Data

Method
  • PFMEA in the AIAG & VDA 7 steps: 1 planning and preparation (header, 5T), 2 structure analysis (process item → process step → process work element by 4M type), 3 function analysis (process item, process step with product characteristic, work element with process characteristic), 4 failure analysis (failure effect on your plant, ship-to plant and end user with severity, failure mode, failure cause), 5 risk analysis (prevention control with occurrence, detection control with detection, Action Priority, special characteristics, filter code), 6 optimization (prevention and detection actions, responsible, target date, status, action taken with evidence, completion date, re-rating) and 7 results documentation.
  • Special characteristics (CC critical, SC significant) are marked in the PFMEA and carried into the control plan. Customer symbols and requirements take precedence.
  • The control plan is generated from the process specification in force for the part (CALOR); edit every row to match the actual practice, and approve it with the cross-functional team.
  • The default PFMEA rows describe typical failure modes of a direct-fired pusher line; they are a starting point to be reviewed and rated by your team.
